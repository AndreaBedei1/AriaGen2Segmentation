# Article 1 — solid-line crossing candidates

**Status: `exploratory_pilot`.**
**No row in this analysis asserts that a traffic rule was broken, and no code
path in the pipeline can produce such an assertion.**

Generated by `scripts/detect_article1_solid_line_candidates.py`. Data:
`solid_line/solid_line_summary.json`, `solid_line/solid_line_candidates.csv`,
review package in `datasets/article1_action_review/`.

## The four permitted states

Automatic detection may emit only `candidate_compliant`,
`candidate_noncompliant`, `uncertain` and `not_evaluable`. A definitive label
requires human review. This is not caution for its own sake: calling a manoeuvre
illegal requires knowing the line was continuous, that the vehicle really crossed
it, and that it was not avoiding an obstacle, turning at a private access, being
directed around roadworks or following a police instruction — none of which is in
a GPS trace or a segmentation mask.

A candidate needs at least three independent kinds of evidence. One segmentation
frame is never enough: a single dropped `lane_marking` mask is a routine model
failure, and an accusation built on it would be built on the model's worst frame.

**GPS cannot resolve a lane crossing in either recording.** The car's fix error
is twenty times the displacement to be measured; even the motorcycle's excellent
3 m fix is 2.5× too coarse. Every lateral excursion the tracker reports is
therefore within its own noise, and the honest state for all of them is
`not_evaluable`.

Reporting "zero violations detected" without that sentence would be badly
misleading — it would read as a statement about the rider.

By manoeuvre context, the excursions were mostly at places where crossing a line
is ordinary anyway: car — 4 roundabout, 1 junction. Motorcycle — 25 junction, 19
roundabout, 12 lane-keeping-or-drift.

## Line continuity does work

Continuity is read from the marking's **duty cycle over time**, not from dash
geometry in a perspective image. A solid line is present in essentially every
frame; a broken one alternates with a period set by the road standard and the
speed. A run too short to distinguish them returns `unknown` rather than
guessing.

| | car block | motorcycle block |
|---|---|---|
| classification | **solid** (conf 0.97) | **solid** (conf 0.99) |
| duty cycle, left / right | 0.97 / 0.84 | 0.95 / 0.99 |

Both 30 s blocks sat alongside a continuous line, correctly identified.

## What does work: lateral position from the image

Since GPS cannot resolve the quantity, the detector also measures lateral
position **from the image**, where both lane boundaries are visible in the near
field and their separation in pixels supplies the metre-per-pixel scale directly,
with no calibration. Frames showing only one boundary get no value: with one
boundary the scale is unmeasured, and guessing it would produce a confident
number from an unmeasured quantity.

| | car | motorcycle |
|---|---:|---:|
| frames with a usable offset | 47 / 300 (16%) | 254 / 450 (56%) |
| offset p05 … p95 | **−0.61 … +0.46 m** | **−1.20 … +1.26 m** |
| occupied band width | **1.07 m** | **2.46 m** |

Over these two 30 s blocks the motorcycle occupies a lateral band **2.3× wider**
than the car within the same lane.

Caveat: the pixel-to-metre scale assumes a 3.5 m lane, so the absolute offsets
scale linearly with that assumption. *Changes* within a block do not depend on
it, and the band-width comparison is only weakly sensitive to it.

The car's 16% coverage is low for two reasons: its block is an urban stretch where
the right-hand boundary is frequently occluded by parked vehicles, **and** the
measurement band was fixed at 0.72–0.95 of the image height, which in a car is
85% dashboard rather than road.

> **Superseded by the full-run proxy.** The band-width figures above come from two
> 30 s blocks with a fixed measurement band. `behavior/lane_position.py` now
> measures the same quantity over the **whole** recording, with the near-field
> road band chosen per recording from the segmentation's own row-wise road
> profile, and with the offset normalised by the lane's own half-width so it does
> not inherit the 3.5 m assumption at all:
>
> | | car | motorcycle |
> |---|---:|---:|
> | measurement band (image rows) | 0.663 – 0.720 | 0.725 – 1.000 |
> | frames with a usable offset | 1 088 / 1 881 (**57.8%**) | 3 497 / 5 023 (**69.6%**) |
> | normalised offset p05 … p95 | −1.13 … +0.50 | −1.06 … +0.82 |
> | **p05–p95 band width** | **1.63** | **1.87** (1.15×) |
>
> On the whole drive the band is 1.15× wider on the motorcycle, not 2.3×, and on
> the 42 shared bins the difference is not distinguishable at all (+0.001 lane
> half-widths, 95% CI [−0.06, +0.16], p_FDR = 0.98). Two 30 s blocks in different
> places were not a basis for the stronger claim. See
> `../article1_final_behavior_statistics/REPORT_MULTIMODAL_COMPARISON.md`.

## Review package

`datasets/article1_action_review/` contains `review_form.csv` (one row per
candidate with empty `human_label`, `human_notes`, `reviewer`, `review_date`),
`candidates.csv`, `manifest.json` and a `README.md` that lists what a reviewer
must check before ever writing `noncompliant`: an obstacle, parked vehicle,
roadworks or emergency vehicle; a junction, private access or roundabout inside
the manoeuvre; a police direction; or the line being a different marking
altogether.

Permitted human labels: `compliant`, `noncompliant`, `uncertain`,
`not_evaluable`.

## No classifier was trained

`classifier_training.attempted: false`. There are no reviewed labels yet, and
training is gated on at least 20 confirmed events per class. Below that the
pipeline refuses. No synthetic events were created to balance anything.

When enough reviewed events exist, the model must be interpretable — logistic
regression, a shallow tree, or gradient boosting with SHAP — split by event or
route group rather than randomly across consecutive frames, and must not receive
frame rate, file name or recording id as a feature.
