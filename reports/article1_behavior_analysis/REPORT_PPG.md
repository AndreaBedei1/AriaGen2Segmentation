# Article 1 — PPG signal quality and heart-rate variability

**Status: `exploratory_pilot`.**
**Interpretation: `physiological_proxy_not_medical`.**

Generated by `scripts/analyze_article1_ppg.py`. Data: `ppg_summary.json`,
`output/.../ppg_beats.parquet`, `ppg_quality.parquet`, `ppg_windows.parquet`.

Nothing in this report is a medical measurement. The sensor is a consumer
photoplethysmograph on a pair of glasses worn by someone riding a motorcycle. No
statement here supports a clinical inference, and an increase in heart rate is
never described as stress.

## The gate

Order matters: every window is assessed before any beat inside it counts, every
beat before it becomes an interval, every interval before it becomes a rate, and
every window's length before it produces a variability figure.

The signal quality index is the geometric mean of three components:

* **band power** — the fraction of spectral energy in 0.5–8 Hz, where a pulse
  lives;
* **template correlation** — mean correlation of each individual pulse with the
  window's average pulse. A clean PPG repeats nearly the same waveform every
  beat; motion noise does not. This is the most informative of the three;
* **interval regularity** — 1 − CV of the intervals in the window.

Motion artefact is judged from the **head IMU**, not from the PPG alone: a motion
artefact and a genuine perfusion change look alike in one channel and quite
different across two.

## The 123 bpm figure, and the check it had to pass

A motorcycle median of 123 bpm against the car's 80 is a large difference, and it
is exactly the shape that a **dicrotic-notch double-detection** produces — the
classic PPG failure, where the secondary pulse wave is counted as a second beat
and the reported rate roughly doubles.

That artefact leaves a specific signature: intervals alternate short–long, so the
lag-1 autocorrelation of the interval series goes strongly **negative**, and
every second peak is much smaller than its neighbours. The detector runs this
check explicitly:

| | car | motorcycle | artefact would give |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| lag-1 interval autocorrelation | **+0.534** | **+0.097** | strongly negative |
| beats below half the local amplitude | 5.3% | 7.9% | ≈50% |
| amplitude coefficient of variation | 0.33 | 0.89 | high, alternating |
| flagged as double detection | no | no | — |

So the elevated rate is **not** that artefact. It is not thereby proven to be a
true heart rate — a systematic detection bias with a different signature would
not be caught — but the most likely instrumental explanation has been tested and
rejected rather than assumed away.

Sustained rates around 120 bpm during motorcycle riding are physiologically
plausible and are reported in the riding literature. This pilot cannot
distinguish "riding elevates heart rate" from "this session, this route, this
day, this rider", and does not try to.

## Heart-rate variability

HRV is refused, with a stated reason, wherever the window cannot support it.
RMSSD needs 30 s, SDNN needs 60 s, pNN50 needs enough intervals to out-resolve
its own 50 ms threshold, and every statistic needs 80% of the window's beats to
have passed the quality gate.

60 s windows stepping 30 s:

| | car | motorcycle |
|---|---:|---:|
| windows | 11 | 32 |
| producing a heart rate | 11 | 30 |
| producing RMSSD | 11 | 30 |
| producing SDNN | 11 | 30 |
| producing pNN50 | 11 | 30 |

Two motorcycle windows were refused entirely, both for the valid-beat fraction.

Short-duration event responses use HR, IBI, pulse amplitude and quality — never
HRV, which is meaningless over a 10 s roundabout.

## On the shared route

The paired comparison over the 42 shared 50 m bins reports the same fact twice,
once in each unit:

| metric | car | motorcycle | difference | 95% CI | Cliff's δ | p (FDR) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| heart rate (bpm) | 79.6 | 121.5 | **+41.3** | [39.8, 45.6] | +1.00 | 0.008 |
| inter-beat interval (ms) | 753.6 | 493.7 | **−254.2** | [−305, −236] | −1.00 | 0.008 |

A Cliff's δ of exactly ±1.00 means complete separation: every motorcycle bin has a
higher heart rate than every car bin. On a spatially paired stretch of the same
road that removes route, road class and speed limit as explanations — and leaves
time of day, traffic, ambient temperature, physical exertion and session, all of
which are confounded with the vehicle here. Separation this complete is a
statement about two sessions, not about two vehicles.

The event-level analysis, which compares each event's change from its own local
baseline rather than an absolute level, finds **no** difference (−5.0 bpm, 95% CI
[−12.5, +1.7], Cliff's δ = −0.20, p_FDR = 0.73, on the 19 events whose baseline
window survived trimming). The between-session level difference is the whole of
the bin-level effect.

## Caveats

* Pulse amplitude depends on LED drive current and integration time as well as on
  perfusion. Both are recorded alongside the value; a change in either rescales
  the signal for instrumental reasons.
* The 10 s quality window is a compromise. A shorter window would track artefacts
  better but gives the template statistic too few beats.
* One participant. Any between-vehicle heart-rate difference is confounded with
  time of day, route, traffic, ambient temperature and everything else that
  differed between two sessions weeks apart.
