# Article 1 — exploratory paired car / motorcycle comparison

**Status: `exploratory_pilot`.**

> One participant, one session per vehicle. These are descriptive statistics of
> two specific drives. They do not estimate a population parameter, they cannot
> be generalised, and no confidence interval here is a statement about drivers in
> general.

Generated by `scripts/compare_article1_paired_route.py`. Data:
`paired/paired_comparison_summary.json`, `paired/paired_route_comparison.csv`,
`paired/paired_statistics.csv`.

> **This report covers the dynamics, physiology and light families.** The full
> comparison — every gaze, blink, pupil and lane-position metric on the same 42
> bins, with three named statistical units — is in
> `../article1_final_behavior_statistics/REPORT_MULTIMODAL_COMPARISON.md`.

## Design

Comparisons run **only** on bins both vehicles drove in the same direction, are
paired on the bin, and take the **bin** as the unit of analysis. A frame is never
treated as an independent sample.

| | |
|---|---:|
| analysis bin size | 50 m |
| paired bins with metrics on both sides | 42 |
| **paired bins with a semantic-gaze reading on both sides** | **42** |
| bootstrap block length | 30 s = 8 bins |
| **effective independent blocks** | **≈ 6** |
| metrics compared | 14 |
| FDR level | 0.05 (Benjamini–Hochberg) |
| surviving FDR | 5 |

**Read the effective sample size, not the bin count.** 42 paired bins along one
continuous stretch of road resolve to about six independent 30 s blocks, and that
is what every interval below rests on.

Intervals come from a **block** bootstrap and tests permute **whole blocks**,
because consecutive samples of a drive are strongly autocorrelated; the i.i.d.
versions would report intervals several times too narrow. `tests/` pins this: a
block bootstrap must not be narrower than the i.i.d. one on autocorrelated data.

## Gaze is now in the comparison

An earlier revision of this report said gaze could not be compared at all, because
the only semantic coverage was two 30 s frozen blocks that did not overlap on the
shared route. That is no longer the case. This comparison consumes the
**full-recording** fast semantic-gaze run, which covers 93.2% (car) and 89.6%
(motorcycle) of each drive, and **all 42 paired bins now carry a gaze reading on
both sides**.

Two gaze metrics appear here, on this script's per-reference-frame estimator. The
per-bin estimator that averages over every gaze sample in the bin — and the
fixation, scanpath, class-group and transition metrics that go with it — is in the
final-statistics report.

**Road complexity index** is built only from map attributes of the bin, and a
paired bin is the *same place* for both vehicles. Its difference is zero by
arithmetic, not by finding. It is kept in the table so nobody recomputes it
expecting something else.

PPG SQI survives here and not in the final-statistics table. The estimate and the
interval are identical; only the multiplicity correction differs, because that
family has 21 metrics and this one has 14. Each p-value is reported with the
family it was corrected in.

## What the five surviving differences mean, and do not mean

All five are in the direction the physical setup predicts for an open vehicle
versus an enclosed one:

* **head vibration +0.22 m/s²** (2.5× the car's median) — a motorcycle transmits
  far more vibration through the body to a head-mounted sensor. This is a
  property of the vehicle and is about as safe as anything here gets.
* **illuminance +4036 lux** — the rider is in the open; the driver is behind
  glass, under a roof, with a head-mounted light sensor pointed into a cabin.
  Again a property of the setup, not of behaviour. It is also why pupil diameter
  has to be light-corrected before it can be compared at all
  (`../article1_final_behavior_statistics/REPORT_BLINK_PUPIL.md`).
* **PPG SQI −0.12** — the motorcycle's higher vibration degrades the optical
  signal. Consistent with the vibration result and with the independently
  measured 4% motion-artefact rate.
* **head angular speed +0.05 rad/s** — the rider moved their head more. Plausibly
  behavioural, but a head-mounted gyro on a vibrating platform also reads more
  angular motion for non-behavioural reasons, and this analysis cannot separate
  the two. Head |yaw rate| specifically does **not** differ, so the extra angular
  speed is not extra lateral scanning.
* **heart rate +42 bpm** — the largest effect and the **least interpretable**.
  The two drives were weeks apart, at different times of day, in different
  traffic and different ambient temperature, with different physical exertion
  before and during. Every one of those is confounded with the vehicle. The
  measurement itself passed its quality gate and its double-detection check (see
  `REPORT_PPG.md`); what it cannot do is attribute the difference to the vehicle.

Speed is **not** significantly different on the shared stretch (+2.2 m/s, p_FDR =
0.61) even though the motorcycle was much faster over its whole route — because
the shared stretch is urban road where both were constrained. That is exactly the
kind of confound that spatial pairing exists to remove, and it worked.

**Gaze road-relevant mass and foveal entropy do not differ** (p_FDR = 0.38 and
0.90). That null holds in the fuller analysis too, on a better per-bin estimator,
and it is the pilot's central gaze result rather than a gap.

## What this comparison cannot support

* Any statement about car drivers or motorcyclists as populations.
* Any causal claim. Two sessions, one participant, no randomisation, no
  counterbalancing.
* Any attribution of the heart-rate difference to the vehicle rather than to the
  session.

## Verdict

| item | grade | reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| pairing method | **A** | map-defined bins, same direction, 2.6 m median centroid separation, every rejection recorded |
| statistical treatment | **A** | block bootstrap, block permutation, FDR, effective sample size reported ahead of row count |
| dynamics / physiology comparison | **C — descriptive only** | five clean effects, but ≈6 independent blocks and one session per vehicle |
| **gaze comparison** | **B — usable with exclusions** | present on all 42 shared bins; the binding limit is ≈6 independent blocks, not coverage |
