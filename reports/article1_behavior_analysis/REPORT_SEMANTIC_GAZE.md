# Article 1 — semantic gaze

**Status: `exploratory_pilot`.**

Generated by `scripts/analyze_article1_semantic_gaze.py`. Data:
`semantic_gaze_summary.json`, `output/.../semantic_gaze.parquet`,
`output/.../gaze_fixations.parquet`. QA renders in `gaze_qa/`.

## Source and the coverage problem

The segmentation is the **frozen Article 1 semantic-camera baseline**
(`feature/article1-motorcycle-ingestion`, commit `a9ee61b`): Mask2Former external
+ Grounding DINO/SAM 2.1 cockpit proxy + fusion + temporal stabilisation + the
bidirectional final pass. It is read, never re-tuned, and **gaze is never fed
back into it** — `tests/test_behavior_guards.py` asserts the gaze module contains
no mask-writing or inference call at all.

That pipeline costs about **27 s per frame** end to end. Running it over both
recordings in full would be roughly 140 GPU-hours, so it exists only for one
dense block per domain:

| | car | motorcycle |
|---|---:|---:|
| block frames | 1801–2100 (300) | 13875–14324 (450) |
| block duration | 29.9 s | 29.9 s |
| **coverage of the recording** | **7.95%** | **2.98%** |
| gaze samples with a foveal reading | 95.0% | 94.0% |

**This is the binding limitation of the whole gaze analysis.** Every number below
describes 30 seconds of driving, not a drive.

## Method

A gaze point is not a pixel. The fovea subtends a couple of degrees, the tracker
has its own error and the segmentation has a boundary of its own; reading one
pixel's label would turn all three uncertainties into a confident wrong answer at
every class boundary.

Each sample is therefore read through a **Gaussian foveal window** (σ = 1.5°,
radius 2.5σ; 15.34 px/degree at the 879 px rectified focal, so σ = 23 px and
radius 58 px) over the class field, weighted additionally by each pixel's own
segmentation confidence. The result is a distribution over classes with its
runner-up and entropy kept.

The frozen baseline stores dense per-class probabilities for only three
diagnostic frames, so this distribution is built from the labels. It is a
*spatial* class distribution over the foveal area, not the model's probability
simplex, and it is named `foveal_*` throughout so the two are never confused.

Fixations are I-VT on the **native 30 Hz** gaze stream, threshold 30 °/s,
minimum duration 100 ms, tolerating gaps up to 75 ms — all durations in seconds,
so the same configuration means the same thing at either frame rate.

## A viewing-geometry bias that had to be corrected

A QA render (`gaze_qa/*.jpg`) shows the projected gaze sitting on the **vanishing
point** — the rider looking far down the road, which is correct driving. But at
that distance the road surface has narrowed to less than the foveal window, so
the surrounding scene wins the argmax:

| | car | motorcycle |
|---|---:|---:|
| road-relevant share, **top-1** | 28.8% | 16.1% |
| road-relevant share, **foveal probability mass** | **37.3%** | **23.8%** |
| `other_environment` mass | 62.5% | 75.5% |

Reporting "4–9% of gaze on `road_surface`" for someone staring straight down the
road would be a statement about perspective, not about attention. Probability
mass does not have that failure mode — a road filling a third of the fovea
contributes a third whether or not it is the argmax — so both are reported and
the mass figures are the ones to read.

`other_environment` is a catch-all (sky, buildings, vegetation) and its dominance
also limits how much the class breakdown can say. A taxonomy that separated sky
from built environment from vegetation would be considerably more informative
here.

takes 31.5% of its foveal mass; the motorcycle block is open road. **These two
blocks are not comparable to each other** — they are different places at
different times — and no car-versus-motorcycle gaze claim is made from them.

## Proxy classes

`mirror`, `instrument_display` and `control_and_ego_vehicle` remain exploratory
proxies. They are flagged `is_proxy_only` and `metric_status:
exploratory_proxy_needs_manual_review` in every per-class record, and the
`off_road_glances` breakdown counts cockpit-proxy runs separately from
out-of-image gaze, because those are different states.

Neither block produced meaningful mirror gaze, so no mirror statistic is offered.

## The gap that matters most

**Neither block sits on the shared route.** Semantic gaze therefore drops out of
the paired car–motorcycle comparison entirely (see `REPORT_PAIRED_AUTO_MOTO.md`).
The fix is mechanical and is the single highest-value next run: execute the
frozen pipeline on a block inside one of the 42 paired bins listed in
`route/paired_route_segments.csv`. At 27 s/frame a 30 s block costs about
2–3.5 GPU-hours per domain.
