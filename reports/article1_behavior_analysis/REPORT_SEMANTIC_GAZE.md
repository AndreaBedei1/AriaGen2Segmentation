## Source and coverage

The segmentation is the full-run Mask2Former/Mapillary fast pipeline, run end to
end over both recordings at a 5 Hz segmentation cadence, with a native-cadence run
kept as a robustness check. Gaze is a **read-only consumer** of those masks and is
never fed back into segmentation — `tests/test_behavior_guards.py` asserts the
gaze module contains no mask-writing or inference call at all.

The residual 6.8% and 10.4% are **not** uncovered road. They are samples with no
valid gaze direction — overwhelmingly blinks: 96.7% (car) and 95.1% (motorcycle)
of gaze-invalid samples coincide with a measured eye closure (see
`../article1_final_behavior_statistics/REPORT_BLINK_PUPIL.md`). A sample taken
through a closed eyelid has nothing to read a semantic class from.

Every gaze sample is associated with the **nearest real segmented frame** by
timestamp, subject to a 120 ms ceiling. Nothing is interpolated and no frame is
synthesised.

## Method

A gaze point is not a pixel. The fovea subtends a couple of degrees, the tracker
has its own error and the segmentation has a boundary of its own; reading one
pixel's label would turn all three uncertainties into a confident wrong answer at
every class boundary.

Each sample is read through a **Gaussian foveal window** (σ = 1.5°, radius 2.5σ)
over the class field, weighted additionally by each pixel's own segmentation
confidence. The result is a distribution over classes with its runner-up and
entropy kept. It is a *spatial* class distribution over the foveal area, not the
model's probability simplex, and is named `foveal_*` throughout so the two are
never confused.

Fixations are I-VT on the **native 30 Hz** gaze stream: threshold 30 °/s, minimum
duration 100 ms, tolerating gaps up to 75 ms — all durations in seconds, so the
same configuration means the same thing at either frame rate.

## Foveal mass, not top-1 share

A QA render (`gaze_qa/*.jpg`) shows the projected gaze sitting on the **vanishing
point** — the rider looking far down the road, which is correct driving. At that
distance the road surface has narrowed to less than the foveal window, so the
surrounding scene wins the argmax. Reporting "4–9% of gaze on `road_surface`" for
someone staring down the road would be a statement about perspective, not about
attention.

Probability mass does not have that failure mode — a road filling a third of the
fovea contributes a third whether or not it is the argmax — so both are reported
and **the mass figures carry every comparison**.

## Comparison on the shared route

Semantic gaze now enters the paired car–motorcycle comparison on **all 42 shared
bins**. The full table is in
`../article1_final_behavior_statistics/REPORT_MULTIMODAL_COMPARISON.md`; the
headline is that **road-relevant gaze mass does not differ** between the two
vehicles on the same 2 100 m of road (−7.4 pp, 95% CI [−10.4, +0.7],
p_FDR = 0.73), while fixation time (−28.6 pp) and scanpath length (+14.6 deg/s)
do.

## Proxy classes and taxonomy limits

`mirror` remains a review-only class and is never written into a primary mask; the
full run reports zero mirror gaze mass and no mirror statistic is offered.
`interior_cockpit` is a coarse "eyes inside the vehicle" indicator that aggregates
pillar, dashboard and tank — it is reported, and it is not a mirror or instrument
metric.

`other_environment` remains a catch-all and still absorbs a large share of foveal
mass. A taxonomy separating sky from built environment from vegetation would make
the class breakdown considerably more informative; the fast taxonomy already
splits those three, which is why the numbers here are more legible than the
earlier block-based ones.
