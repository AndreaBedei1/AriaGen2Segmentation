# Article 1 — vehicle dynamics and head dynamics

**Status: `exploratory_pilot`.**

Generated by `scripts/analyze_article1_dynamics.py`. Data:
`dynamics_summary.json`, `output/.../vehicle_dynamics.parquet`,
`output/.../head_dynamics_native.parquet`.

## The separation, and why it is absolute

The only inertial sensors in these recordings sit on the temples of a pair of
glasses. They measure gravity, plus how the head moves on the neck, plus how the
vehicle shakes the whole assembly — three things superimposed. Reading a
longitudinal vehicle acceleration off that signal would be wrong in a way that
looks entirely plausible.

So there are two families, computed by different functions, from different
sensors, into different tables, and every row of each carries a column saying
which of the two it is (`measures`, `derived_from_head_imu`,
`is_vehicle_acceleration`). `tests/test_behavior_analysis.py` asserts that
`compute_vehicle_dynamics` contains no reference to an accelerometer or gyro at
all.

## A. Vehicle dynamics

Source priority is `vio_pose → gps_speed_field → gps_position_derivative`. There
is no usable pose stream in either recording, so both fall through to the GPS
receiver's own speed field, and `speed_source` records that on every row.

The position-derivative speed is always computed as an independent cross-check
even when it is not the chosen source:

| | car | motorcycle |
|---|---:|---:|
| speed source | `gps_speed_field` | `gps_speed_field` |
| cross-check correlation with position derivative | **0.850** | **0.978** |
| median speed | 10.4 m/s (37 km/h) | 15.2 m/s (55 km/h) |
| 95th percentile speed | 15.4 m/s | 26.8 m/s |
| maximum speed | 15.5 m/s | 33.1 m/s |
| acceleration p05 / p95 | −0.92 / +0.93 m/s² | −1.48 / +1.69 m/s² |
| estimated lateral acceleration p95 | 2.07 m/s² | 3.17 m/s² |
| time stopped | 0.0 s | 12.0 s |

The car's weaker cross-check (0.85 against 0.98) is the poor GPS again: its
position derivative is noisy because its positions are.

Lateral acceleration is **estimated** as `v² × κ` with curvature read from the
road geometry, not measured. It is what the road demands at that speed, not what
the vehicle experienced.

### What the sampling rate can and cannot see

Both recordings report **zero hard-braking episodes** at the −2.5 m/s²
threshold. That is a detection limit, not a driving observation, and the report
says so on the row:

* **car** — the most negative acceleration a 1 Hz speed series resolves here is
  −1.32 m/s², above the threshold. Hard braking is not detectable at all.
* **motorcycle** — the threshold *was* touched (−2.64 m/s²) but never for the
  0.5 s an episode requires. At a 1 s sampling interval that needs two
  consecutive samples below threshold, so brief hard braking registers as a
  single sample and is not counted.

A 1 Hz speed series averages a brake application over a whole second. Recovering
braking events needs either a higher-rate speed source or a pose stream; neither
exists in this data.

## B. Head dynamics

Gravity is estimated as the low-passed accelerometer vector (0.3 Hz) and
subtracted. The check that this worked is the recovered magnitude:

| | car | motorcycle | expected |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| median gravity magnitude | 9.852 m/s² | 9.863 m/s² | 9.80665 m/s² |

Within 0.6% in both recordings.

Yaw, pitch and roll are resolved against physical directions rather than
device-axis names: **up** is the gravity direction, **forward** is the RGB
optical axis projected perpendicular to it, **right** is the camera's lateral
axis. Roll specifically needs that third reference — built from the optical axis
and gravity alone it is identically zero, because both vectors span the same
plane. (That was a real bug, caught by the roll series reading exactly 0.0°.)

| | car | motorcycle |
|---|---:|---:|
| median angular speed | 0.144 rad/s | 0.187 rad/s |
| **vibration RMS, median** | **0.141 m/s²** | **0.357 m/s²** |
| vibration RMS, p95 | 0.331 m/s² | 0.827 m/s² |
| lateral head checks per minute | 10.7 | 9.1 |
| relative head roll p05…p95 | −3.7…+5.4° | −6.4…+3.4° |

The motorcycle transmits **2.5× the vibration** of the car to the rider's head at
the median and 2.5× at the 95th percentile. This is the clearest and least
ambiguous difference in the whole pilot, and it is a property of the vehicle, not
of the rider.

Absolute head pitch and roll carry a large constant offset — the RGB sensor is
mounted rotated on the device and the glasses sit at some angle on a particular
face — so only the posture-relative angles, taken against each recording's own
median, are comparable across recordings. Both are reported, with the absolute
ones explicitly named `*_absolute_with_mounting_offset`.

## C. Head–eye coordination

Generated by `scripts/analyze_article1_final_behavior.py`; data in
`output/article1/final_behavior_statistics/head_eye_summary.json`.

Head checks are found on the head IMU's own ~805 Hz samples, because a 0.15 s
excursion is three samples at the gaze rate and would be lost there. Gaze
excursions are found on the 30 Hz gaze stream. A gaze excursion is
**head-assisted** when a lateral head check overlaps it within 0.30 s and
**eye-only** otherwise.

| | car | motorcycle |
|---|---:|---:|
| lateral head checks | 67 (10.7 /min) | 152 (9.1 /min) |
| median head-check duration | 0.371 s | 0.333 s |
| gaze lateral excursions | 63 (10.1 /min) | 198 (11.8 /min) |
| **eye-only scans** | 29 (4.6 /min) | 113 (6.7 /min) |
| **head-assisted scans** | 34 (5.4 /min) | 85 (5.1 /min) |
| head-assisted fraction | **54.0%** | **42.9%** |
| head-check left / right asymmetry index | +0.075 | +0.092 |
| gaze-excursion left / right asymmetry index | +0.143 | +0.081 |
| gaze counter-rotating against head motion | 73.3% | 68.6% |

Head-assisted scans occur at the same rate in both vehicles (5.4 vs 5.1 /min),
while eye-only scans are 46% more frequent on the motorcycle (6.7 vs 4.6 /min).
The motorcycle rider makes more lateral gaze shifts, and the extra ones are made
with the eyes alone. That is consistent with the shared-route finding that head
|yaw rate| does **not** differ between the vehicles while scanpath length does.

Both vehicles show a slight leftward asymmetry in both signals (index +0.07 to
+0.14, where 0 is symmetric and +1 all-left). This is a left-hand-drive country
with left-side overtaking; it is reported, not explained.

Gaze counter-rotates against head motion on 69–73% of samples where the head is
moving. That is the expected vestibulo-ocular response — the eyes holding a target
while the head turns — and it is instrumentation-level physiology, not a
behavioural finding.

### What a lateral head check is not

**It is not a mirror check.** This signal is a sustained yaw excursion of the
head. A head turn to a mirror, to a side road, to a shop window and a head turn
because the vehicle leaned are identical in it. Every count above is named
`lateral_head_check`, the summary carries `is_mirror_check_count: false`, and
`tests/test_final_behavior_statistics.py` asserts that no artefact relabels it.

## Caveats

* Head vibration is a *head* measurement. It reflects the vehicle, the
  suspension, the rider's posture and the helmet together; it is not a
  measurement of the vehicle's own vibration.
* Lateral head checks are counted from a yaw-rate threshold. A head turn to check
  a mirror and a head turn to look at scenery are indistinguishable here, and no
  output attributes one to a mirror.
* The eye-only versus head-assisted split depends on two thresholds and a
  concurrency tolerance, all recorded on the summary. It is a description of two
  measured signals, not of intent.
* All speed statistics inherit the GPS quality of their recording. The car's are
  the weaker.

| acceleration metrics | **C — descriptive only** | a 1 Hz speed series cannot resolve the events that matter; zero braking counts are detection limits |
