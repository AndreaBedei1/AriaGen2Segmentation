# Article 1 — multimodal car / motorcycle comparison

**Status: `exploratory_pilot`.**

> One participant, one session per vehicle. These are descriptive statistics of
> two specific drives. They do not estimate a population parameter, they cannot
> be generalised, and no confidence interval here is a statement about drivers in
> general.

Generated by `scripts/analyze_article1_final_behavior.py` and
`scripts/compare_article1_final_paired.py`. Data: `paired_statistics.csv`,
`paired_bin_metrics.csv`, `event_type_medians.csv`,
`paired_comparison_summary.json`, and
`output/article1/final_behavior_statistics/`. Figure
`figures/12_paired_gaze_physiology_summary.png`.

## What changed, and why every gaze number in this directory is new

The earlier behaviour analysis had semantic gaze for two 30 s frozen blocks — one
per vehicle — and **the two blocks did not overlap on the shared route**, so no
paired bin carried a gaze reading on both sides and gaze dropped out of the
car/motorcycle comparison entirely.

That limitation is gone. The fast full run segments **every** recording end to
end, and its semantic gaze covers the whole drive:

| | car | motorcycle |
|---|---:|---:|
| gaze samples | 11 283 | 30 138 |
| samples with a foveal semantic reading | 10 514 | 27 018 |
| **coverage of the recording** | **93.2%** | **89.6%** |
| **shared 50 m bins with gaze on both sides** | **42 / 42** | **42 / 42** |

The residual 7% and 10% are blinks and tracking dropouts, not uncovered road: a
gaze sample without a valid gaze direction has nothing to read a semantic class
from, and 95–97% of those are explained by eye closure (see
`REPORT_BLINK_PUPIL.md`).

## Design

| | |
|---|---:|
| analysis bin size | 50 m |
| paired bins driven by both vehicles in the same direction | 42 |
| paired bins with metrics on both sides | 42 |
| bootstrap block length | 30 s = 8 bins at 12.9 m/s |
| **effective independent blocks** | **≈ 6** |
| metrics compared, bin family | 21 |
| surviving FDR, bin family | **9** |
| FDR level | 0.05 (Benjamini–Hochberg, within each family) |

**Read the effective sample size, not the bin count.** 42 paired bins along one
continuous stretch of road resolve to about six independent 30 s blocks, and that
is what every interval below rests on.

Three families of comparison, each with its own explicitly named unit. **The frame
is never a unit**, and `tests/test_behavior_guards.py` pins that.

* **bin** — the 42 map-defined 50 m bins. Paired on the bin, which is the same
  place for both vehicles, so road class, curvature and speed limit are held
  constant by construction. This is the primary family.
* **event** — one row per road event, unpaired, comparing each event's *change
  from its own local baseline* rather than an absolute level, because the two
  drives do not share events and a level difference would carry the whole
  between-session gap.
* **time block** — 30 s blocks of the whole recording, for signals with no spatial
  anchor. 13 car blocks, 34 motorcycle blocks.

Intervals come from a **block** bootstrap and tests permute **whole blocks**,
because consecutive samples of a drive are strongly autocorrelated and the i.i.d.
versions would report intervals several times too narrow.

## The result that matters most is a null

**Road-relevant gaze mass does not differ between the two vehicles on the shared
route** (−7.4 percentage points, 95% CI [−10.4, +0.7], Cliff's δ = −0.17,
p_FDR = 0.73). Neither does the top-1 version of the same quantity, nor foveal
entropy, nor the top-1 distribution entropy.

This is the comparison the whole pilot was built to make and it was previously
impossible to attempt. Its answer is: on 2 100 m of the same road, driven in the
same direction, **the share of foveal attention on road-relevant classes is not
distinguishable between the two vehicles.**

That null is worth more than it looks, because it constrains what the differences
that *do* survive can mean. Attention is not going to different places. It is
being *deployed differently in time*:

* **fixation time falls by 28.6 percentage points** on the motorcycle (81% → 51%),
  the second-largest effect in the whole table (δ = −0.83);
* **scanpath length rises by 14.6 deg/s** (+38%);
* **blink rate rises by 18/min** (+74%);
* semantic transition rate rises by 1.4/s, though not past FDR.

Same targets, less dwelling, more moving between them. All four are in the same
direction and the two strongest are the two most directly about fixation stability.

There is a competing explanation and this analysis cannot exclude it: the
motorcycle transmits **2.5× the head vibration** (+0.225 m/s², δ = +0.99, the
cleanest effect in the pilot), and a vibrating head produces gaze-velocity
excursions that an I-VT fixation classifier reads as saccadic. Less measured
fixation time on a vibrating platform is expected for instrumental reasons alone.
Separating "the rider fixated less" from "the platform shook the measurement"
needs a fixation classifier validated under vibration, which this pilot does not
have.

## The other surviving differences

* **interior-cockpit gaze mass −0.74 pp** (0.85% → 0.00%). The car driver spends a
  small but non-zero share of foveal mass on their own vehicle's interior; the
  motorcycle rider's median is exactly zero. This is close to a definitional
  result — a motorcycle has almost no interior to look at — and is reported as a
  sanity check on the semantic pipeline as much as a behavioural finding.
* **head angular speed +0.05 rad/s.** Plausibly behavioural, but a head-mounted
  gyro on a vibrating platform also reads more angular motion for
  non-behavioural reasons, and this analysis cannot separate the two. Note that
  head |yaw rate| specifically does **not** differ (+0.004, p_FDR = 0.98): the
  extra angular speed is not extra lateral scanning.
* **blink rate +18/min**, reproduced independently at +23/min on 30 s time blocks
  (δ = +0.87, p_FDR = 0.008). Two units of analysis agreeing is the strongest form
  this pilot can offer. What causes it — airflow, visor, light, session — is not
  identifiable here.
* **pupil residual −0.039 mm** after the light correction. This one does **not**
  reproduce on time blocks (−0.008 mm, p_FDR = 0.64), and 0.04 mm is at the edge
  of what a 2.2 mm measurement from this device supports. Treat as descriptive.
* **heart rate +41 bpm / IBI −254 ms** are the same fact twice and remain the
  least interpretable: the two drives were weeks apart, at different times of day,
  in different traffic and different ambient temperature, with different physical
  exertion. Every one of those is confounded with the vehicle. The measurement
  passed its quality gate and its double-detection check (`REPORT_PPG.md`); what
  it cannot do is attribute the difference to the vehicle.
* **head vibration +0.225 m/s²** is a property of the vehicle, not of the rider,
  and is about as safe as anything here gets.

**Speed is not different on the shared stretch** (+2.2 m/s, p_FDR = 0.64) even
though the motorcycle was much faster over its whole route, because the shared
stretch is urban road where both were constrained. That is exactly the confound
spatial pairing exists to remove, and it worked.

## Event family

40 car events and 184 motorcycle events, comparing each event's change from its
own local baseline to its immediate window.

Windows come from `behavior/events.build_windows` — the same function the
road-event analysis uses — so each has one anchor chosen by the sign of its start
offset, is clipped to the recording, and is trimmed away from a neighbouring
manoeuvre that would contaminate it. That trimming bites hard here: the motorcycle
has 62 junctions and 13 roundabouts in 1 004 s, so **81% of baseline windows and
84% of delayed windows are clipped**, and the median baseline is 3 s rather than
the requested 20 s. Every clip is recorded on its row with a reason, and an event
whose baseline was trimmed to nothing produces no delta rather than a silent zero.
That is why `n` below is 15–23 rather than 40.

Events are not independent either: two junctions 5 s apart on the same road share
their traffic, their light and their rider. The bootstrap block spans the same 30 s
decorrelation length used everywhere else, which is **10 events** here.

**Nothing survives FDR in the event family.** Two rows have intervals that exclude
zero while their permutation p-value does not reject — blink duration and gaze
entropy. On 15–19 paired units with a 10-unit block that disagreement is expected
rather than informative: a block permutation of two blocks has very few
distinguishable arrangements, so its p-value cannot go low however large the
effect. Neither is offered as a finding.

The per-type breakdown in `event_type_medians.csv` shows the underlying problem:
3–10 events per type for the car, and exactly one traffic signal in the whole
pilot. Those medians are reported for completeness and are not estimates of
anything.

measures it over 30 s. Two units of analysis with very different quantisation
give the same answer.

## Robustness: native versus 5 Hz

The primary analysis segments at 5 Hz. The native-cadence run is the robustness
check, and the two agree to within the third significant figure on every
recording-level attention metric:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| valid gaze fraction | 0.9318 | 0.9319 | 0.8965 | 0.8966 |
| road-relevant mass | 34.84% | 34.92% | 33.19% | 33.15% |
| interior-cockpit mass | 6.64% | 6.45% | 1.44% | 1.46% |
| foveal entropy | 0.2338 | 0.2360 | 0.2487 | 0.2498 |
| fixation time | 75.97% | 75.97% | 53.85% | 53.84% |
| scanpath | 41.55 °/s | 41.55 °/s | 53.49 °/s | 53.50 °/s |

Segmenting at 5 Hz rather than at the native camera cadence does not change any
conclusion in this report. Neither run was modified by this analysis.

## What this comparison cannot support

* Any statement about car drivers or motorcyclists as populations.
* Any causal claim. Two sessions, one participant, no randomisation, no
  counterbalancing.
* Any attribution of the heart-rate, blink-rate or pupil differences to the
  vehicle rather than to the session.
* Any conclusion that the motorcycle rider fixated less **as a behaviour**, as
  distinct from the platform's vibration degrading the fixation measurement.

## Verdict

| item | grade | reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| pairing method | **A** | map-defined bins, same direction, 2.6 m median centroid separation, every rejection recorded |
| statistical treatment | **A** | block bootstrap, block permutation, FDR within family, effective sample size reported ahead of row count, three named units and never the frame |
| **gaze comparison** | **B — usable with exclusions** | 42/42 shared bins now carry gaze on both sides; the binding limit is ≈6 independent blocks and one session per vehicle, not coverage |
| dynamics / physiology comparison | **C — descriptive only** | nine clean effects, but ≈6 independent blocks and one session per vehicle |
| event-related comparison | **D — must be repeated** | nothing survives FDR; 3–10 car events per type is not enough to attempt it |
| fixation-time difference | **C — descriptive only** | large and consistent, but confounded with vibration degrading the fixation classifier |
